\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This study sized a PV--battery micro-grid for Faqra Club, with the existing diesel generators kept
as backup, using an hourly linear program built on public data, a bottom-up load model and a
measured record of feeder availability. The least-cost design combines 976~kWp of PV with a
2,623~kWh / 786~kW battery and serves the whole load in every hour. It cuts the annualized supply cost
by 40\%, fuel use by 83\% and CO$_2$ emissions by 691~t/yr. The investment of \$1.24M has a simple
payback of 3.8 years, an NPV of \$2.33M and a real IRR of 25.8\%. The NPV remains positive over the
whole range of diesel prices from 0.60 to 2.00~\$/L, and net metering would raise it further.

Three findings carry beyond this site. First, under an unreliable grid, PV and storage are valuable
mainly because they displace diesel, which explains the short payback compared with resorts on reliable
grids. Second, winter sets the size of the system, and the summer surplus is either curtailed or,
once export terms are set, sold. Third, a loss-of-load allowance is a poor saving at a seasonal
resort: an optimizer places the shedding in the peak season, where it costs the club most.

The results are scenario estimates. Metered load data, the actual ratings of the existing gensets and
transformers, and the final net-metering terms are needed before detailed design. The open MATLAB
model can be re-run as these data become available and reused for other seasonal tourism sites.
