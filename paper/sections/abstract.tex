\begin{abstract}
Faqra Club, a ski resort at 1,735--1,975~m in Lebanon, relies on a national grid that supplies power
about 8~h per day and on diesel generators for the rest. This paper assesses the feasibility of a
PV--battery micro-grid that keeps the existing generators as backup. The hourly load is
reconstructed bottom-up from the club's facilities, and grid availability is taken from a year of
measured feeder records. A linear program in MATLAB sizes the PV array and the battery and schedules
all sources over 8,760 hours, minimizing the annualized cost without shedding any load. The optimal
design combines 976~kWp of PV with a 2,623~kWh / 786~kW lithium iron phosphate battery. It reduces
the annualized supply cost by 40\%, fuel use by 83\% and CO$_2$ emissions by 691~t/yr. The investment
of \$1.24M has an NPV of \$2.33M at a 7.11\% real discount rate, a real IRR of 25.8\%, and a simple
payback of 3.8 years, and the NPV stays positive for diesel prices from 0.60 to 2.00~\$/L. Allowing
load shedding saves little, because the optimizer places it in the ski season, while net metering
would raise the saving to 54\%. A single-line diagram with islanding at the 20~kV point of common
coupling and prioritized load zones completes the design.
\end{abstract}
